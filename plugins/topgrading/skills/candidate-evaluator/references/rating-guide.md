# Candidate Rating Guide

## A/B/C Player Classification

### A Player Definition
**Top 10% of talent available for the role at the compensation offered**

Characteristics:
- Consistently exceeds expectations
- Would enthusiastically rehire
- Others want to work with/for them
- Drives results independently
- Elevates team performance
- Strong cultural fit
- All competencies meet or exceed MAR

**Hire Decision**: Strong Yes

---

### B Player Definition
**Capable performer but not in top 10%**

Characteristics:
- Meets most expectations
- Competent but not exceptional
- May need significant development
- Some competencies below MAR
- Potential to become A Player with coaching
- Or consistent B Player (acceptable in some roles)

**Hire Decision**: Maybe - depends on role requirements and development capacity

---

### C Player Definition
**Does not meet expectations for the role**

Characteristics:
- Performance below requirements
- Multiple competencies significantly below MAR
- Would not rehire
- May have red flags (integrity, reliability, etc.)
- Poor cultural fit
- Significant concerns from reference checks

**Hire Decision**: No

---

## Competency Rating Scale

### 4 - Excellent (Top 10%)
- Exceptional performance in this competency
- Consistently demonstrates at the highest level
- Would be a role model for others
- Clear, compelling behavioral evidence

**Evidence looks like**:
- Multiple specific examples
- Quantifiable results
- Consistent across situations
- Others specifically mention this strength

### 3 - Very Good (Above Average)
- Strong performance in this competency
- Meets high standards consistently
- Room for some growth
- Solid behavioral evidence

**Evidence looks like**:
- Good examples with clear outcomes
- Generally consistent performance
- Some variation across situations
- Recognized as a strength

### 2 - Good (Meets Requirements)
- Adequate performance in this competency
- Meets basic expectations
- Noticeable room for development
- Some behavioral evidence

**Evidence looks like**:
- Basic examples provided
- Inconsistent results
- Learning curve evident
- Not a notable strength or weakness

### 1 - Weak (Below Expectations)
- Performance below requirements
- Significant development needed
- Limited behavioral evidence
- May be a significant gap

**Evidence looks like**:
- Vague or no examples
- Poor results when attempted
- Clear pattern of weakness
- Others have noted concerns

## Multi-Evaluator Calibration

### When Multiple Interviewers Rate
1. Each interviewer rates independently first
2. Compare ratings for significant discrepancies (2+ point differences)
3. Discuss specific evidence driving different ratings
4. Reach consensus rating based on combined evidence
5. Document reasoning for final rating

### Averaging Ratings
- Final rating = Average of evaluator ratings, rounded
- If average falls on .5, round toward the rating with stronger evidence
- Document any significant disagreements

## Red Flags to Watch For

### Integrity Red Flags
- Inconsistent stories across the interview
- Blaming others for all failures
- Reluctant to have references contacted
- Unexplained gaps in employment
- Exaggerating accomplishments (verified through TORC)

### Performance Red Flags
- Pattern of short tenures (< 18 months)
- Declining trajectory in career
- Vague about specific results
- No quantifiable achievements
- Managers "let them go"

### Cultural Fit Red Flags
- Values misalignment
- Negative about all past employers
- Poor self-awareness
- Resistant to feedback
- "Not my job" mentality

### Motivation Red Flags
- Unclear career goals
- Only motivated by money
- Running away from something vs. running toward opportunity
- Low energy or enthusiasm
- Misaligned expectations

## Cost of Mis-Hire Awareness

When evaluating borderline candidates, consider the cost of getting it wrong:

| Role Level | Cost of Mis-Hire | Multiple of Base Salary |
|------------|------------------|------------------------|
| Entry Level | $50,000 - $100,000 | 1-2x |
| Professional | $100,000 - $250,000 | 3-5x |
| Manager | $250,000 - $500,000 | 5-8x |
| Director | $500,000 - $1,000,000 | 8-15x |
| Executive | $1,000,000 - $5,000,000 | 15-27x |

### Cost Components
1. **Hiring costs** - Recruiting, interviewing, onboarding
2. **Compensation** - Salary, benefits, equity during tenure
3. **Management time** - Supervision, coaching, performance management
4. **Mistakes and failures** - Errors, missed opportunities, rework
5. **Disruption** - Team morale, customer impact, lost momentum
6. **Severance** - Exit costs, legal fees, unemployment
7. **Replacement** - Starting the hiring process over

**Key Insight**: It's almost always better to keep searching than to hire a B or C Player.
